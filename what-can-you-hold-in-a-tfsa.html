What Can You Hold in a TFSA? Stocks, ETFs, GICs and More

A TFSA can hold a lot more than cash. Here is everything the Canada Revenue Agency lets you hold in one, each in one line. The short version: almost anything you would buy through a bank or a broker.

What you can hold

  • Cash. Money sitting in the account, earning whatever interest the bank pays.
  • Savings deposits. A TFSA savings account at a bank or credit union.
  • GICs. Guaranteed investment certificates that lock your money in for a set term at a set rate.
  • Government bonds. Canada and provincial bonds, including savings bonds.
  • Corporate bonds. Bonds issued by companies.
  • Mutual funds. Pooled funds run by a fund company, holding a mix of stocks and bonds.
  • Segregated funds. Insurance-company funds that work much like mutual funds, with some insurance features.
  • ETFs. Exchange-traded funds that trade on a stock exchange like a share, often tracking an index.
  • Stocks. Shares of companies listed on a designated stock exchange, such as the Toronto Stock Exchange, the New York Stock Exchange or NASDAQ.
  • REIT units. Units in a real estate investment trust, which owns property and pays out the rent it collects.
  • Options and warrants. Contracts tied to listed shares, if they are qualified investments.
  • Certain small-business shares. Shares of some small Canadian private companies, under strict CRA rules.

Two things worth knowing. First, a TFSA at a bank may only offer cash and GICs — to hold stocks and ETFs you need a self-directed TFSA at a brokerage. Second, US dividends inside a TFSA lose 15% to US withholding tax, and you cannot get it back.

What you cannot hold

  • Cryptocurrency directly. You cannot hold bitcoin or other coins in a TFSA, but a crypto ETF listed on a designated exchange is allowed.
  • Land and property. You cannot hold a house, a condo or a plot of land in a TFSA.
  • Art, jewellery and collectibles. These are not qualified investments.
  • Shares of a private company that are not qualified. Most shares in a private company do not qualify.

Holding something that does not qualify can cost you tax on it, so check with the CRA list of qualified investments or your provider before you buy.

What is the best TFSA?

There is no single best TFSA — the best TFSA for you depends on what you want to hold. If you want a safe place for cash, a bank TFSA with a good savings rate or a GIC may be enough. If you want stocks or ETFs, you need a self-directed TFSA at a brokerage. Pick the account that holds what you want, with fees you are happy with.

Common questions

Can I hold stocks in a TFSA?

Yes. You can hold shares of companies listed on a designated stock exchange, such as the TSX, NYSE or NASDAQ. You need a self-directed TFSA at a brokerage — a bank TFSA usually only offers cash and GICs.

Can I hold cryptocurrency in a TFSA?

No, not directly. You cannot hold bitcoin or other coins in a TFSA. A crypto ETF that is listed on a designated stock exchange is allowed.

Can I hold a GIC in a TFSA?

Yes. GICs are qualified investments for a TFSA, and most banks offer TFSA GICs.

Can I hold US stocks in a TFSA?

Yes. US stocks listed on the NYSE or NASDAQ are allowed. Note that US dividends in a TFSA lose 15% to US withholding tax, which you cannot recover.
