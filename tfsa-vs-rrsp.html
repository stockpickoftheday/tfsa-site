TFSA vs RRSP: Which Is Better for You?

A TFSA and an RRSP are both tax shelters, but they work in opposite ways. An RRSP gives you the tax break when you put money in and taxes you when you take it out. A TFSA gives you no break going in, and never taxes you again. For many Canadians, that makes the TFSA the better first account.

The benefits of a TFSA over an RRSP

  • Withdrawals are truly free. You never pay tax on TFSA withdrawals, and they do not count as income. RRSP withdrawals are taxed in full, in the year you take them out.
  • Your room comes back. Take $5,000 out of a TFSA and you get $5,000 of room back the next January 1. Take $5,000 out of an RRSP and that room is gone forever.
  • No earned income needed. Students, stay-at-home parents and retirees can all build TFSA room. An RRSP needs earned income to create room.
  • No age limit. You can contribute to a TFSA at any age. An RRSP must be turned into a RRIF or an annuity, or withdrawn, by December 31 of the year you turn 71. (Budget 2026 proposed ending that rule, but as of November 2026 it is only a proposal, not law.)
  • Withdrawals do not cut your benefits. TFSA withdrawals do not affect Old Age Security or the Guaranteed Income Supplement. RRSP withdrawals can push your income high enough to claw back OAS.
  • Any goal, not just retirement. A TFSA works for a house, a car, a trip or an emergency fund. An RRSP is built for retirement, with only narrow exceptions like the Home Buyers’ Plan.

When an RRSP still wins

A TFSA is not better in every case. If you are in a high tax bracket now and expect a much lower one in retirement, the RRSP’s upfront tax deduction can be worth more than the TFSA’s tax-free withdrawal. An employer RRSP match is also free money you should not turn down. Many Canadians do best with both: the RRSP for the tax deduction in high-income years, the TFSA for everything else.

Common questions

Is a TFSA better than an RRSP?

It depends on your income and your goals. A TFSA wins on flexibility: withdrawals are tax-free, room comes back the next year, there is no age limit, and withdrawals do not affect OAS or GIS. An RRSP wins when you are in a high tax bracket now and expect a lower one in retirement, because its contributions are tax-deductible.

Can I have both a TFSA and an RRSP?

Yes. Most Canadians who can afford it hold both. A common approach is the RRSP for the tax deduction in high-income years and the TFSA for everything else.

Do TFSA withdrawals affect my Old Age Security?

No. TFSA withdrawals are tax-free and do not count as income, so they cannot trigger an OAS clawback. RRSP withdrawals do count as income and can affect OAS.

What is the RRSP limit for 2026?

The 2026 RRSP dollar limit is $33,810, or 18% of your 2025 earned income, whichever is lower. The TFSA limit for 2026 is $7,000, the same for everyone.
