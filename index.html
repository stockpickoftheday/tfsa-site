What Is a TFSA and How Much Room Do You Have?

A TFSA (Tax-Free Savings Account) is a registered account where your money grows tax-free and comes out tax-free. It is not just a savings account — you can hold stocks, ETFs, bonds, GICs and mutual funds inside one. Enter your date of birth below to find your total TFSA contribution room.

Where the TFSA came from

Finance Minister Jim Flaherty introduced the Tax-Free Savings Account in the 2008 federal budget, tabled on February 26, 2008. It came into effect on January 1, 2009, starting with a $5,000 yearly limit. The idea was a flexible account for any purpose — the Canada Revenue Agency calls it “an RRSP for everything else in your life” — where money grows tax-free and withdrawals are never taxed.

What you can hold

  • Cash. Money sitting in the account, earning whatever interest the bank pays.
  • Savings deposits. A TFSA savings account at a bank or credit union.
  • GICs. Guaranteed investment certificates that lock your money in for a set term at a set rate.
  • Government bonds. Canada and provincial bonds, including savings bonds.
  • Corporate bonds. Bonds issued by companies.
  • Mutual funds. Pooled funds run by a fund company, holding a mix of stocks and bonds.
  • Segregated funds. Insurance-company funds that work much like mutual funds, with some insurance features.
  • ETFs. Exchange-traded funds that trade on a stock exchange like a share, often tracking an index.
  • Stocks. Shares of companies listed on a designated stock exchange, such as the Toronto Stock Exchange, the New York Stock Exchange or NASDAQ.
  • REIT units. Units in a real estate investment trust, which owns property and pays out the rent it collects.
  • Options and warrants. Contracts tied to listed shares, if they are qualified investments.
  • Certain small-business shares. Shares of some small Canadian private companies, under strict CRA rules.

TFSA contribution limit by year, 2009 to 2026

The TFSA limit 2026 is $7,000, unchanged from 2024 and 2025. Someone who was 18 or older and a resident of Canada every year since 2009, and never contributed, has $109,000 of total room in 2026. The 2027 limit has not been published yet.

Common questions

What is a TFSA?

A TFSA is a Tax-Free Savings Account: a registered account where your money grows tax-free and comes out tax-free. You can hold cash, GICs, stocks, ETFs, bonds and mutual funds inside one. Contributions are not tax-deductible.

What is the TFSA contribution limit for 2026?

The TFSA contribution limit for 2026 is $7,000. Your total room is bigger than that if you have unused room from earlier years: someone eligible since 2009 who never contributed has $109,000 of room in 2026.

How do I find my exact TFSA contribution room?

Check CRA My Account — it shows the room the CRA has on file for you. This calculator shows your maximum room if you have never contributed; subtract what you have put in and add back what you withdrew in earlier years.

Do TFSA withdrawals count as income?

No. Withdrawals are tax-free and do not count as income. They also do not affect federal income-tested benefits like Old Age Security or the Guaranteed Income Supplement. The amount you withdraw comes back as new room on January 1 of the next year.
