TFSA Rules, Made Easy

The rules of a Tax-Free Savings Account in short form. No tax on what goes in? No — you pay no tax on what grows or comes out. Here is the whole set, in plain words.

Who can open one

  • You are 18 or older.
  • You are a resident of Canada for tax purposes.
  • You have a valid Social Insurance Number (SIN).

Your room starts building up in the year you turn 18, even if you have not opened an account yet. In provinces and territories where the age of majority is 19 — British Columbia, New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, Nova Scotia, the Northwest Territories, Nunavut and Yukon — you can still build up room from 18, but you cannot open the account until you turn 19.

TFSA contribution limit 2026

The TFSA contribution limit for 2026 is $7,000. Unused room carries forward forever, so your total room can be much larger than one year’s limit. Someone who was 18 or older and a resident every year since 2009, and never contributed, has $109,000 of room in 2026. Use the calculator to find your own maximum room.

Tax

  • Contributions are not tax-deductible. You get no tax break when you put money in.
  • Growth is tax-free. Interest, dividends and capital gains inside a TFSA are not taxed.
  • Withdrawals are tax-free. You pay no tax when you take money out, and withdrawals do not count as income.
  • Withdrawals do not affect your benefits. Taking money out of a TFSA does not reduce Old Age Security, the Guaranteed Income Supplement or other income-tested benefits.

TFSA withdrawal rules

You can take money out of a TFSA any time, for any reason, with no tax and no penalty. The amount you withdraw is added back to your contribution room on January 1 of the next year — not right away. The common mistake is taking money out and putting it back in the same year without enough room left: that is an over-contribution, and it is taxed.

The over-contribution penalty

If you put in more than your room allows, the Canada Revenue Agency charges 1% per month on the highest excess amount, for every month it stays in the account. Check your room in CRA My Account before you put in a large amount.

Other rules worth knowing

  • No maximum age. There is no age limit, and no forced withdrawals — unlike an RRSP, which must be converted by the end of the year you turn 71.
  • No deadline. There is no contribution deadline. You can contribute any day of the year.
  • Non-residents cannot contribute. If you become a non-resident of Canada, contributions you make while non-resident are taxed at 1% per month.
  • Move money by transfer, not withdrawal. To move money between TFSAs, ask the new provider to transfer it directly. If you withdraw and re-deposit, the money only comes back as room the next January 1.
  • Day trading can be taxed. Buying and selling shares often inside a TFSA can be treated as business income, which is taxable. The CRA looks at how often you trade and how you do it.

Common questions

What is the TFSA contribution limit for 2026?

The TFSA contribution limit for 2026 is $7,000. Your total room is the sum of every year's limit since you became eligible, minus what you have contributed, plus what you withdrew in earlier years.

What happens if I over-contribute to my TFSA?

The CRA charges a tax of 1% per month on the highest excess amount for every month it stays in the account. Take the excess out as soon as you notice it.

Can I withdraw from my TFSA and put the money back the same year?

Only if you have enough unused room. The amount you withdraw only comes back as new room on January 1 of the next year. Putting it back in the same year without room is an over-contribution.

Do TFSA withdrawals affect my Old Age Security?

No. TFSA withdrawals are tax-free and do not count as income, so they do not affect Old Age Security, the Guaranteed Income Supplement or other income-tested benefits.
